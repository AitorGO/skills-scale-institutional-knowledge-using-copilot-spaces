# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

This folder contains the project management knowledge used by OctoAcme. It is designed to centralize process guidance, reinforce common practices, and make it easier for teams to align on how work is planned, executed, communicated, and improved.

## Overview of OctoAcme project management processes

OctoAcme follows a structured, customer-first project management model built around clear ownership, iterative delivery, and measurable outcomes. Each project is expected to define a business need, identify stakeholders, set success metrics, and move through a repeatable lifecycle: initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospectives. The organization emphasizes transparency, accountability, and cross-functional collaboration so work is visible and decisions stay aligned to business value.

The process begins with project initiation, where teams validate the problem, align stakeholders, define success criteria, and decide whether to proceed with planning. Planning then turns the idea into a prioritized backlog with scope, milestones, timelines, risks, and a clear definition of done. During execution, teams work in short cycles using regular standups, project tracking, and risk escalation to keep delivery visible and coordinated. Release management ensures the solution is validated, deployed safely, and communicated clearly to stakeholders. At the end of a milestone or release, retrospectives capture lessons learned and convert them into action items for continuous improvement.

OctoAcme also defines clear roles and responsibilities across the project lifecycle. Product managers define outcomes and priorities, project managers coordinate planning and communication, developers build and test the solution, QA validates quality and acceptance criteria, and stakeholders provide input, approvals, and business context. Communication is treated as a first-class practice through regular standups, weekly updates, milestone reviews, and escalation paths for blockers and cross-team dependencies. Quality is reinforced through CI checks, security scanning, unit and integration testing, smoke tests, and review gates before release.
